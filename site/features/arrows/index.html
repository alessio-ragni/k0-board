Arrows between notes

Draw arrows between Claude Code sessions on the k0 board, across repositories, to show which story waits on which — then close or finish a whole chain at once.

Work is joined up, and a board of separate notes does not say so

A handover that needs a fix in another repository first. A release that waits on three stories. With a dozen sessions open, which of them belong together is something you carry in your head — until you draw it. An arrow goes from the note that waits to the note it waits on, and it can leave one repository's column and land in another's.

Why it matters

The arrow is the same fact k0 already used for waits on: while the story at the far end is not Done, the one that waits carries a small chain mark and asks once before it is started. It is a note to yourself, never a lock. What is new is that you draw it with the pointer, you can see the whole chain at a glance, and you can act on the chain as one thing.

How to use it

  • Put the pointer on a note: an arrow mark appears at the bottom right, left of the sun. Press it and drag onto another note, then let go. The board slides when you near an edge, so a note three columns away is reached by dragging towards it.
  • The board at rest shows no arrows. A note that has some keeps its mark lit, with the count. Put the pointer on it and its whole chain is drawn; click the mark and it stays until you click elsewhere.
  • Click an arrow for two words: Flip turns it round, Remove takes it away.
  • On the note the arrows leave, Close offers to close the sessions of the stories it waits on, and Done offers to mark them done too. Only this one is always the other answer.
  • In a terminal, /k0-arrows tells Claude what the story of that session is joined to — in any repository, with where each one stands and what its log last said — and draws, removes or turns an arrow when you say so: link this to K1154.

Small print

  • An arrow that would leave stories waiting on each other for ever is refused, and k0 says which stories: K12 already waits on K7.
  • A finished story keeps its arrows, faded. An arrow shows only when both its notes are on the board, so with Done hidden those are gone. A deleted story takes its arrows with it.
  • Closing or finishing a chain follows the arrows the way they point, never back up them, and leaves alone any session that is working.
  • Arrows need the backlog switched on. The list view writes them as waits on in the panel beside a row instead of drawing them.
