Find any story with ⌘K

Press ⌘K on the k0 board to find any Claude Code session by its number, a word of its title or its notes — then jump to its terminal or open it.

One box over the whole board

With a dozen repositories open the board is wider than the screen, some columns have folded away for being quiet, and a filter may be hiding half of it. Somewhere in there is the story you want to check on. ⌘K — Ctrl+K on Windows and Linux — opens a box over the board, wherever you are, and finds it for you.

Type its number, 42 or K42, with or without the K. Or a word of its title, or a word you remember from its notes. Half a word is enough, and so are scattered letters: expo finds Export, qfind finds Quick find. Several words narrow it down, because every one of them has to be there.

Why it matters

The question you ask most with many sessions running is what is that one doing? Hunting for its note across three screens of columns is the slow way to answer it. The box goes straight to the answer: Enter takes you to that session's terminal, the same as a double click on its note.

How to use it

  • ⌘K opens it, Esc shuts it, the arrows move through the results.
  • Enter, or a double click on a row, is Terminal: the session's window up front if it is running, the conversation it left behind if it was closed, a new session if there was never one.
  • ⌘Enter is Open: the story itself, to edit its title or its notes.
  • Both are also buttons on every row.
  • With nothing typed, it lists the open stories you touched last — so ⌘K and Enter is the way back to whatever you were just on.

Small print

  • Finished stories are found too, after everything still open and in grey. They have no terminal to go to, so there Enter opens them.
  • With the backlog on, repositories come up as well, and choosing one filters the board on it.
  • It searches what the board already has on screen, so typing asks nothing of anyone — and a story in a folded column or behind a filter is found all the same.
