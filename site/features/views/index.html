Kanban to work, List to plan

k0 shows your Claude Code stories two ways — Kanban, a column of notes per repository, and List, every epic, story and task as rows to plan with.

Since k0 0.5.0

One switch, two shapes

The switch in the top bar, in among the filters, says Kanban or List. It changes the shape of what you are looking at and never what you are looking at: the state pills, the repository and the epic lane apply to both, so nothing appears or vanishes when you switch.

Kanban is for working. The board you already know — one column per repository, notes that reorder themselves by who is waiting for you. With a backlog behind it a note grows four marks and no more: its key, like K42; its epic, as a small label in the epic's own colour; a flag in the bottom corner; and a warning when something it waits on is not finished.

List is for planning. The same stories as rows, every repository at once: a heading per repository, epics that open and close with their progress bar, stories under them, and tasks indented beneath the story they were cut from. A row carries key, title, state, flag, how many decisions are still standing and how much of the checklist has passed.

Why two

Working and planning ask different questions. While working it is which one needs me now, and a board of coloured notes answers that from across the room. While planning it is what is left, and in what order, and that wants everything at once, in rows you can run your eye down.

How to use it

  • Click Kanban or List in the top bar.
  • Hover a note and click the flag in its bottom corner. Once on, the note takes a border in the flag's colour, so you can find it from across the room; click again to take it off.
  • Click an epic's label and the board becomes that epic: a strip under the top bar shows a progress bar, 4 of 11 done, and the way back out. It survives a reload.
  • In List, click a row and the story opens beside it — its rounds, decisions, plan, checklist and log — so the rows you were comparing stay on screen.
  • Drag a row to reorder it, or onto another epic to move it there.
  • Open a story with the pencil to keep notes on it: they sit on the post-it for you and are never sent to Claude Code.

Small print

Progress counts the epic's stories and is not weighted: eleven stories with one done is 1 of 11. Dragging never changes state — states move from the terminal and the Done button. And the two views come with the backlog: with "backlog": false in ~/.k0/config.json the switch leaves the bar.
